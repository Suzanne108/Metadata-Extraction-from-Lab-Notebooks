   Content: ISA in plain language

- ISA is a simple way to organize and share the metadata that describes an experiment: what project it is, what was done, and what the results were. It helps other researchers understand, reproduce, and reuse the work, and it makes data easier to find and compare.

- The core idea is three linked levels:
  - Investigation: the project-wide context (why, who, where, and what publications come from it).
  - Study: a coherent experimental unit within the project (specific aims, variables, protocols, materials, and planned measurements).
  - Assay: the actual measurement or observation made in a study (the data produced, plus the specifics of how that measurement was done).

Key components and how they relate

- Investigation → Studies → Assays
  - Investigation is the top container. It holds the project title, description, people, publications, and the list of Studies.
  - Each Study is a self-contained part of the project. It has its own title/identifier, description, and a set of elements like factors, protocols, materials, and one or more Assays.
  - Each Assay is a concrete measurement or observation arising from a Study. It links back to the Study, and it records what was measured, how, with which materials, and the data files produced.

Common objects you’ll see in papers and data

- Protocols
  - A protocol is a defined, repeatable set of steps used in an experiment. It can include parameters, components, and optional descriptions of how to perform a procedure.

- Protocol parameters
  - Specific inputs to a protocol (e.g., temperature, time, reagent concentrations). They help other researchers reproduce the exact conditions.

- Processes and process parameters
  - A Process is one instance of carrying out a step defined by a protocol, usually acting on a material or sample. Process parameters are the actual values used in that instance.

- Materials (and subtypes)
  - Source: the starting biological material (e.g., a tissue or organism) used at the outset.
  - Sample: a material produced by applying a protocol to a Source (a typical "output" used in an assay).
  - Material: other kinds of materials used in the study that aren’t sources or samples (e.g., reagents, extracts).
  - Material attributes and values describe characteristics like origin, type, or quality.

- Factors and Factor values
  - Factor: an experimental variable (e.g., genotype, treatment, time point) that is deliberately varied.
  - FactorValue: the specific value a Factor has in a given context (e.g., “treated” vs “control”; 24 hours).

- Data files
  - DataFile entries capture the results of assays (raw data, derived data, images, spectra, etc.). They are linked to the Assay that produced them.

- Ontology annotations and sources
  - OntologyAnnotation: a term from a controlled vocabulary (e.g., GO terms, experimental method terms) to standardize meaning.
  - OntologySourceReference: where that term comes from (which ontology and version).
  - These help ensure everyone uses the same language for concepts like “cell type,” “measurement,” or “method.”

- People, organizations, and publications
  - Person and Organization capture who did the work and where they are affiliated.
  - Publication links to journal articles, DOIs, or PubMed IDs related to the Investigation or Study.

- Samples, descriptions, and attributes
  - Samples carry characteristics and may have derived values (factor values) used in assays.
  - Attribute/value structures provide a consistent way to describe characteristics across materials.

Semantic Functions by Object

- Investigation
  - Role: the project’s umbrella, describing scope, goals, and who/where. Connects to Studies, Publications, and People.
  - Information it carries: title, description, lists of studies, people, publications, and ontology sources that define terms used in the project.

- Study
  - Role: a coherent experimental unit within the Investigation. Connects to Protocols, Materials, Processes, Assays, and Factors.
  - Information it carries: identifier, title, description; protocols applied; materials used (sources, samples, other materials); sequence of processes; associated assays; experimental factors.

- Assay
  - Role: a specific measurement or observation tied to a Study. Connects to DataFiles, Materials, and Process sequences.
  - Information it carries: measurement type, technology type, data files produced, materials involved (samples/other materials), and steps (process sequence) used to obtain results.

- Protocol
  - Role: a defined procedure that can be reused across Studies.
  - Information it carries: name, type, description, parameters, and any components involved in the steps.

- ProtocolParameter
  - Role: a defined input to a Protocol.
  - Information it carries: parameter name and allowed values or ranges.

- Process
  - Role: a concrete execution of a protocol step, applied to materials or samples.
  - Information it carries: the protocol step it implements, the materials/samples involved, and parameter values used.

- ProcessParameterValue
  - Role: the actual values used in a Process instance.
  - Information it carries: parameter name and specific value used.

- Source
  - Role: the starting biological material for the study.
  - Information it carries: name, characteristics (attributes), and references to origin.

- Sample
  - Role: an intermediate or final material produced in a Study, used in an Assay.
  - Information it carries: name and a set of characteristics; may include factor values.

- Material
  - Role: other materials used in a Study (reagents, extracts, etc.).
  - Information it carries: name, type (e.g., Extract Name), and characteristics.

- MaterialAttribute / MaterialAttributeValue
  - Role: describe the characteristics (categories) of materials.
  - Information it carries: category (attribute type) and value (the actual characteristic), plus optional unit and source.

- Factor / FactorValue
  - Role: the variables that are deliberately varied in experiments.
  - Information it carries: factor name/type and the values used in a study or assay.

- Person / Organization
  - Role: identify who did the work and where they are affiliated.
  - Information it carries: name, contact details, affiliations, roles.

- Publication
  - Role: link to scholarly outputs related to the Investigation or Study.
  - Information it carries: title, authors, identifiers (DOI, PubMed), etc.

- OntologyAnnotation / OntologySourceReference
  - Role: provide standardized terms and their origins for controlled vocabularies.
  - Information it carries: term value, source name, term accession, and reference to the ontology source.

- DataFile
  - Role: store the outputs of assays (data, results, etc.).
  - Information it carries: file type/name, and linkage to the related Assay.

What to look for when reading a paper (ISA-oriented checklist)

- Project and scope
  - Do you see a clear Investigation with a project title, description, and authors/affiliations?

- Experimental design and units
  - Are there Studies that define the experimental unit, aims, and design? Look for study titles, identifiers, and descriptions.

- Variables and conditions
  - Are the Factors and FactorValues described? Note what variables were changed and at which levels or time points.

- Protocols and methods
  - Are Protocols named and described? Are ProtocolParameters and any ProtocolComponents listed so you could reproduce the steps?

- Materials and samples
  - Are Sources (starting materials), Samples, and other Materials described with characteristics? See if Materials have Attribute/Value pairs.

- Procedures and execution
  - Do you find Process sequences and ProcessParameterValues showing how steps were executed on materials?

- Measurements and data
  - Are Assays linked to DataFiles? Do you see measurementType and technologyType for the assay?

- Ontologies and terminology
  - Are terms annotated with OntologyAnnotation and sourced from an OntologySourceReference? Do you see controlled vocabulary usage?

- Data availability and provenance
  - Are there links to publications, data repositories, and data files? Do you see submission/public release dates?

- Reproducibility details
  - Are enough details provided (protocol steps, parameter values, materials) to reproduce the study or re-use the data?

Practical takeaway

- ISA is a way to capture “what was done, how, with what, and what was produced” in a way that other researchers (or machines) can understand and reuse.
- Think of Investigation as the project-level spine, Study as the experimental unit, and Assay as the concrete measurement with the resulting data.
- Use the common objects (protocols, materials, factors, data files, ontology terms) to describe every part of the work consistently.
