What ISA is and why it’s useful
- ISA stands for Investigation, Study, and Assay. It’s a simple, structured way to describe what was done in life-science research, what was measured, and what produced the results.
- It helps you compare experiments, reuse data, and connect methods to results across papers and projects. By organizing metadata at three levels, you can see project goals, specific experimental units, and the measurements that came out of them.

Key components and how they relate
- Investigation: the top-level project context. It can hold a title, description, people involved, publications, and links to related Studies.
- Study: a coherent experimental unit inside an Investigation. A Study groups together its own design descriptors, protocols, materials, processes, and the assays that come from applying them.
- Assay: a specific measurement or readout in a Study. An Assay ties to the protocols that produced the measurement, the materials involved, and the data files or outputs generated.
- How they connect: Investigation contains Studies; each Study contains Protocols, Materials, Processes, and Assays. Assays link to the data they produced. Protocols define the steps; Processes are concrete executions of those steps on inputs to produce outputs.

Common objects you’ll see and what they do
- Protocol: a written plan of steps for a procedure. It provides the blueprint that gets instantiated as a Process.
- Protocol Parameter: a category of settings used in a Protocol (e.g., temperature, time). It defines what can be varied.
- Process: an instance of a Protocol being carried out. It lists inputs (what was used) and outputs (what was produced), and can include date, performer, and parameter values.
- Process Parameter Value: a concrete value used when a Protocol is executed (e.g., “temperature = 37°C”). It records how a Protocol was performed.
- Material: the items involved in the Study that aren’t strictly Source or Sample. It includes things like Extract Names or other materials used or produced.
- Material Attribute and Material Attribute Value: describe characteristics or properties of materials (the “characteristics” that define a category, and the specific value for a given material).
- Source: the starting biological material (e.g., organism, tissue) used in the Study.
- Sample: a material produced by a Protocol or used as an input to a later step; often a more concrete item like an extract, cell pellet, or labeled sample.
- Factor and Factor Value: experimental variables (like genotype, treatment, timepoint). Factors describe what is being varied; Factor Values assign specific conditions for a Study or Assay.
- Data File: the actual data outputs from an Assay or a Process (raw data, processed results, or files linked to measurements).
- Ontology Annotation and Ontology Source Reference: controlled terms for describing things (e.g., organism, tissue, assay type). OntologySourceReference describes which ontology is used; OntologyAnnotation ties a term to its source and accessions.
- Publication, Person, Organization: metadata about authors, affiliations, and the scholarly outputs related to the Investigation or Study.
- Data relationships: DataFiles, Samples, Sources, and Materials can be inputs or outputs of Processes and Assays; Protocols provide the steps that connect inputs to outputs; Ontology annotations provide standardized terms for concepts like organism or tissue.

Semantic functions by object
- Investigation: carries the project umbrella—title, description, people, Publications, ontology references, and the list of Studies it contains.
- Study: defines a coherent experimental unit with its own design descriptors, related Publications, a set of Protocols, Materials, and Processes, plus the Assays and Factors that describe how measurements were made and under what conditions.
- Assay: records a single measurement event or readout; points to the Measurement Type, Technology Type, and the Protocols used; lists data files and materials involved for that measurement.
- Protocol: the plan or method to be followed; not an execution itself but a blueprint that is instantiated as a Process.
- Protocol Parameter: a named setting within a Protocol that can be recorded as part of how the Protocol is applied.
- Process: the concrete execution of a Protocol on specific inputs to produce outputs; includes inputs, outputs, performer, date, and any parameter values that customized the Protocol.
- Process Parameter Value: the actual setting used in a Process for a given Protocol Parameter; pairs a parameter with a concrete value.
- Source: the starting material; describes what was used at the beginning of the study (and can have its own characteristics).
- Sample: a concrete output or intermediate material produced by a Protocol; can derive from a Source and be used as input for later steps.
- Material: general items used or produced in the Study that aren’t strictly Source or Sample; often used for things like Extract Name or other materials with defined characteristics.
- Material Attribute and Material Attribute Value: categorize and record the specific characteristics of a Material (e.g., tissue type, extraction method) and the actual values for those characteristics.
- Factor and Factor Value: explain what conditions were varied in the Study; Factor Values give the exact setting for each Experiment (e.g., a specific drug dose).
- Data File: stores the results of an Assay or a Process; may include links to raw and processed data.
- Ontology Annotation and Ontology Source Reference: provide controlled vocabulary terms and their sources; this makes metadata interoperable and easier to search.
- Publication, Person, Organization: capture authorship, affiliations, and scholarly context for traceability and credit.

What to look for in typical life-science articles (ISA-aware checklist)
- Project framing: does the paper clearly state the study’s aims and what larger Investigation it belongs to (title, description, authors, affiliations)?
- Experimental units: are Studies defined so you can see separate experimental units or aims within the larger project?
- Protocols and procedures: are the planned methods described in enough detail to be reproduced? look for named protocols or steps that might be instantiated as Processes.
- Materials and samples: can you identify sources (starting materials), samples, and any other materials used? note any characteristics or attributes described for these materials.
- Experimental variables: are Factors and Factor Values described (e.g., genotype, treatment, dose, timepoint)?
- Protocol execution and parameters: is there a record of how the Protocol was actually applied (Process with inputs/outputs, Performer, Date, and Process Parameter Values)?
- Measurements and assays: what was measured, with which technology or measurement type? check Assays for data files and links to Protocols used.
- Data outputs: are Data Files described (raw and processed data) and linked to their Assays? are data repositories or file names provided?
- Ontology use: are terms annotated using controlled vocabulary (Ontology Annotations) and is the ontology source referenced (Ontology Source Reference)?
- Relationships and provenance: can you trace how inputs moved through Processes to Outputs, and how data relate back to the original Sources/Materials?
- Publications and credits: are related publications and author affiliations listed to provide provenance and context?

Semantic Functions by Object (concise recap)
- Investigation: top-level project context; contains Studies, Publications, People, and Ontology references.
- Study: the unit of work that groups protocols, materials, processes, and assays; defines design and variables.
- Assay: a single measurement event; ties to data files and the protocols/materials used to obtain the measurement.
- Protocol: the plan; describes the steps to perform a procedure.
- Protocol Parameter: a selectable setting within a Protocol that can be recorded for reproducibility.
- Process: a real-world execution of a Protocol on specific inputs to produce outputs; includes dates, performers, and parameter values.
- Process Parameter Value: the actual value used for a Protocol Parameter in a given Process.
- Source: the starting biological material used in the Study.
- Sample: a material produced or used in the workflow; often a concrete output of a Protocol.
- Material: items used or produced that aren’t strictly Source or Sample; includes extracted or intermediate materials.
- Material Attribute Value: the concrete characteristics of a Material.
- Material Attribute: the category of a Material’s characteristic.
- Factor Value: the specific condition/value for a Factor in a Study or Assay.
- Factor: the variable being tested or varied (e.g., treatment, genotype).
- Person: an individual involved in the project (author, contributor, etc.).
- Organization: institutions or groups associated with People or publications.
- Publication: papers or outputs linked to the Investigation or Study.
- Ontology Annotation: a controlled term used to describe an element with a standardized meaning.
- Ontology Source Reference: the source of the ontology term (which ontology, version, etc.).
- Data File: the actual data produced by the Assay or Process.

Practical tip
- When reading a paper, slowly map sections to the three ISA levels: project context (Investigation), experimental units (Studies), and measurements (Assays). Then identify the protocols and their execution (Process and Process Parameter Values), the materials and their characteristics (Source, Material, Material Attribute Values), and the results (Data Files) along with any ontology annotations.
