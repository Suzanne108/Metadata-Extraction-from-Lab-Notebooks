   Content: What ISA is and why it’s useful
- ISA (Investigation/Study/Assay) is a simple way to organize experiment metadata so people and machines can understand how a project was done, what was measured, and what everything relates to.
- It provides a consistent structure for describing project context, experimental design, procedures, materials, data, and terms from controlled vocabularies.
- Using ISA helps you track provenance, reproduce experiments, compare studies, and share metadata alongside your data.

Key components and how they relate
- Investigation: the top-level container for a project. It holds the project title, description, people, publications, ontology references, and the collection of Studies.
- Study: a coherent experimental unit within an Investigation. Each Study has its own identifier, title, description, study design descriptors, protocols, materials, processes, factors, and the set of Assays that come from that Study.
- Assay: a concrete measurement or observation tied to a Study. It records what was measured, how (measurement type and technology), the protocols used, the materials involved, any data files produced, and its place in the study’s process sequence.

Common objects you’ll see
- Protocols: predefined procedures or methods that can be reused across Studies and Assays. They may have parameters and components (building blocks used in the protocol).
- Protocol parameters: the adjustable values that tailor a protocol for a given run.
- Processes: practical executions that apply a Protocol to inputs to produce outputs. They show the actual flow of work (inputs, outputs, when it happened, who performed it).
- Materials: things used or produced in the study, including:
  - Sources (origin material, e.g., a tissue source or organism source),
  - Samples (the material that is assayed, e.g., a extracted sample),
  - Other Materials (extracts or reagents that are not sources or samples).
- Material attributes: characteristics describing Materials (e.g., organism, tissue type) and their values.
- Factors: experimental variables or conditions (e.g., genotype, treatment).
- Data files: outputs of assays, which can be raw or derived data and may come in various formats.
- Ontology annotations and ontology sources: controlled terms to standardize descriptions (e.g., a term from an ontology with its source reference).
- People, Organizations, Publications: who did the work, where it was done, and how to cite it.
- Data (DataFile) and related concept terms for data type designation and provenance.

Semantic functions by object
- Investigation: provides project-wide context and provenance. It aggregates publications and people, and links to all Studies within the project.
- Study: organizes a coherent set of experiments. It lists study design descriptors (describing how the study is planned), and ties together protocols, materials (sources, samples, other materials), processes, and assays.
- Assay: captures the observable outcome of a Study. It records what was measured (measurement type), how it was measured (technology), the data files produced, and the inputs/outputs via its process sequence.
- Protocol: defines a repeatable set of steps or methods. It serves as the blueprint that Studies and Processes reference to perform work.
- Protocol Parameter: documents the fine-tuning knobs of a Protocol so others can reproduce the exact conditions.
- Process: records the execution of a Protocol on inputs to produce outputs. It shows the workflow and can chain with previous/next Processes to reflect the full experimental flow.
- Process Parameter Value: specifies the actual values used in a given Process execution.
- Source: the origin material that enters the study, such as an organism or tissue source.
- Sample: the material that is directly assayed.
- Material (and Attribute/AttributeValue): non-source materials like extracts or reagents; attributes describe those materials with defined values.
- Factor and FactorValue: define and record the experimental variables and their specific values used in a Study or Assay.
- DataFile: the actual data produced, which can be linked to an Assay.
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference: provide standardized terms and their sources, improving interoperability and searchability.
- Publication, Person, Organization: help identify who did the work, where it was done, and how to cite it.

What to look for in typical life-science papers (checklist)
- Project context: is there an overarching project title/description that frames the investigation?
- Studies: are there clearly defined experimental units with identifiers, titles, and descriptions?
- Experimental design: are study designs described (design descriptors, control/treatment groups, randomization, replication)?
- Factors and values: are experimental variables listed as Factors with specific FactorValues (e.g., genotype, dose, timepoint)?
- Protocols: are key methods described or referenced to specific Protocols (name, type, version, URI if available)?
- Materials: are there descriptions of Sources and Samples, plus any Other Materials (reagents, extracts)? are material attributes described (e.g., tissue type, organism strain)?
- Processes and data flow: is there a sequence of Processes showing how protocols were executed, inputs/outputs, and who performed them?
- Assays and data: are Assays described with measurement types and technologies, and are associated DataFiles provided or referenced?
- Ontologies: are terms standardized with OntologyAnnotations and sources, improving clarity and reuse?
- Contributors and affiliations: are People and Organizations listed, with publications linked for citations?
- Reproducibility cues: are exact conditions, parameters, and data file formats recorded to enable replication?

Semantic Functions by Object (full list)
- Investigation: top-level project container; holds title, description, submissions/publications, people, studies, and ontology references.
- Study: unit of coherent experiments; includes identifiers, title, description, studyDesignDescriptors, protocols, materials (sources, samples, other materials), processSequence, assays, factors, and optional comments.
- Assay: measurement/observation record tied to a Study; includes measurementType, technologyType, dataFiles, materials (samples and otherMaterials), processSequence, and optional descriptors.
- Protocol: reusable set of steps; includes protocolType, name, description, parameters, components, and references for reuse.
- ProtocolParameter: a named adjustable value used by a Protocol.
- Process: protocol execution instance; references an executable Protocol, lists parameterValues, inputs and outputs (which can be Sources, Samples, Data, or Materials), performer, date, and surrounding Process links.
- ProcessParameterValue: concrete values for a Process’s parameters.
- Source: origin material; part of Materials; provides lineage for how a Sample was obtained.
- Sample: material that is directly analyzed by an Assay.
- Material: non-source materials such as extracts or reagents used in the workflow; includes name, type (e.g., Extract Name), and characteristics.
- MaterialAttribute and MaterialAttributeValue: descriptors for Materials’ characteristics (e.g., tissue type, organism).
- Factor and FactorValue: experimental variables and their concrete values used in the Study or Assay.
- Person: individual contributors.
- Organization: institution or group involved.
- Publication: citable references describing the work.
- OntologyAnnotation: controlled-term description with possible termSource and termAccession.
- OntologySourceReference: provenance for an ontology term (which ontology and where the term came from).
- DataFile: a Data object associated with an Assay (e.g., raw/derived data, image files, matrices).
