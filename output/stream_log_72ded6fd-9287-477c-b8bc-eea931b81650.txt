What ISA is and why it’s useful
- ISA stands for Investigation/Study/Assay. It’s a simple, structured way to describe the metadata (the “what, how, and with what data”) behind life-science experiments.
- The goal is to capture enough context so someone else can understand, reproduce, or reuse the work. It links project-level context to specific experiments and the data they produce.

Key components and how they relate (Investigation → Studies → Assays)
- Investigation: the top-level project context. It holds the overall title, description, who was involved, related publications, and an organized list of studies.
- Study: a coherent experimental unit within the project. A Study describes the design, variables, protocols used, materials (inputs), and the sequence of processes that were applied.
- Assay: a concrete measurement or readout performed for a Study. An Assay records what was measured, how (measurement type/technology), what materials were measured, any data files produced, and the relevant procedures.
- How they connect: An Investigation contains multiple Studies; each Study can include multiple Protocols, Materials, Processes, Factors, and one or more Assays. Assays are the data-producing endpoints that tie back to the Study and, ultimately, to the Investigation.

Common objects (and how they fit)
- Protocols: standardized procedures or sets of steps used in experiments. Protocols can have parameters and components that specify inputs or sub-steps.
- ProtocolParameter: adjustable values used by a Protocol (e.g., temperature, time, reagent concentration). They define how a Protocol is executed in a particular context.
- Process: an instantiation of steps from a Protocol as it is actually carried out on materials. It is the running sequence that turns inputs into outputs.
- ProcessParameterValue: the actual values used for a specific Process step (e.g., 37°C, 10 minutes).
- Materials: inputs to a Study (and sometimes outputs). They include:
  - Source: the original biological material (starting point).
  - Sample: a material that results from applying a Protocol to a Source (e.g., a treated tissue sample).
  - Other materials: extracts or constructs described as Materials (e.g., “Extract Name” or “Labeled Extract Name”).
- Factors: experimental variables that are altered or tested (e.g., genotype, treatment dose). Each Factor can have associated FactorValues describing the specific condition.
- FactorValue: the concrete value for a Factor in a given context (e.g., “genotype: X,” “dose: 5 uM”).
- DataFiles (Data): outputs from an Assay. This includes raw data files and derived results (e.g., sequence files, spectra, processed results).
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference: controlled terms used to describe concepts (terms, categories, measurements) and the sources of those terms. They help standardize language across studies.
- Publication: references to the scientific articles that describe or relate to the Investigation or Studies.
- Person and Organization: people and institutions involved (e.g., authors, funders, facilities).
- Sample: a material that is described in detail within a Study (often tied to Factors and DataFiles).
- Source: the starting biological material (the true origin of samples).
- Publication, OntologySourceReference, OntologyAnnotation: ensure terms and references are precise and reusable.

Semantic functions by object (what each object does, how it connects, and what it carries)
- Investigation
  - Role: project-wide container for context, links to related Studies, publications, people, and ontologies.
  - Connections: contains Studies; may reference Publications, People, and OntologySources.
  - Information carried: title, description, identifiers, submission/public release dates, and references.
- Study
  - Role: a coherent experimental unit within the project.
  - Connections: contains Protocols, Materials (Sources, Samples, Other Materials), ProcessSequence, Assays, Factors, and Publications.
  - Information carried: title, description, studyDesignDescriptors (ontology annotations), lists of materials and protocols, factors, and any associated data.
- Assay
  - Role: the actual measurement event(s) for a Study.
  - Connections: linked to a Study; lists dataFiles; references to measurementType and technologyType; may include Protocols, Processes, and Materials (Samples/Other Materials).
  - Information carried: measurementType (what’s measured), technologyType (how it’s measured), dataFiles (outputs), materials used (samples or other materials), and process sequence.
- Protocol
  - Role: a reusable recipe or set of steps used in experiments.
  - Connections: referenced by Studies and sometimes by Assays (via process sequences); contains ProtocolParameters and Components.
  - Information carried: protocolType (the kind of procedure), description, version, parameters (ProtocolParameter), and components (sub-steps or inputs).
- ProtocolParameter
  - Role: a configurable input to a Protocol.
  - Connections: parts of a Protocol.
  - Information carried: parameter name and value (or allowed ranges) used in a particular execution.
- Process
  - Role: an actual run of Protocol steps on materials.
  - Connections: part of a Study’s processSequence; uses Materials and Protocols; can be linked to ProcessParameterValue.
  - Information carried: the instantiated steps and their order.
- ProcessParameterValue
  - Role: the concrete settings for a Process step.
  - Connections: belongs to a Process.
  - Information carried: the specific values used (e.g., 5 minutes, 37°C).
- Source
  - Role: starting material in a Study.
  - Connections: used by Samples or Materials; can have characteristics and be linked to a Study.
  - Information carried: name, characteristics, and other descriptive details.
- Sample
  - Role: a material produced by applying a Protocol to a Source (an output material that is measured in an Assay).
  - Connections: linked to a Study, to DataFiles via Assays, and to FactorValues.
  - Information carried: name, characteristics, and associated FactorValues.
- Material
  - Role: inputs or outputs that are not Source or Sample (e.g., Extracts, Labeled Extracts).
  - Connections: used in Studies and Assays; has characteristics.
  - Information carried: name, type (e.g., Extract Name, Labeled Extract Name), characteristics.
- MaterialAttribute and MaterialAttributeValue
  - Role: describe characteristics of Materials.
  - Connections: attach to Materials; used to specify details like properties or conditions.
  - Information carried: attribute name and value.
- Factor and FactorValue
  - Role: describe experimental variables and the concrete values used.
  - Connections: defined in a Study; FactorValues are the specific settings for a given context (often tied to Samples or Data).
  - Information carried: factorName, factorType, and the concrete value(s).
- DataFile (Data)
  - Role: outputs of Assays; where results are stored.
  - Connections: linked to Assays; may be raw or derived data.
  - Information carried: name, type (e.g., Raw Data File, Derived Data File, etc.).
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference
  - Role: standardize terms and provide sources for those terms.
  - Connections: annotate keys like measurement types, protocol types, or characteristics; reference sources for terms.
  - Information carried: termValue, termSource, termAccession; source reference details (name, version).
- Publication, Person, Organization
  - Role: credits, contacts, and affiliations; provide provenance and context.
  - Connections: Publication links to Investigation/Study; Person and Organization appear as authors, contacts, or affiliations.
  - Information carried: names, identifiers, affiliations, emails, etc.

What to look for in typical life-science articles (concise checklist)
- Project and scope
  - Look for a clear project title and a description that points to a broader investigation.
  - Note any publications that are tied to the project and the main contacts or institutions involved.
- Studies and design
  - Identify the Studies within the project: what was tested or compared, and what variables were varied.
  - Check for studyDesignDescriptors that describe the experimental design (e.g., factorial design, time-course).
- Protocols and steps
  - Look for explicit Protocols or references to standard procedures used.
  - See if ProtocolParameters and Components are described, so you can understand the exact steps and inputs.
- Materials and samples
  - See what Sources were used (starting materials) and what Samples were produced or analyzed.
  - Note any other Materials (e.g., extracts) and any MaterialAttributes that describe their properties.
- Experimental variables
  - Identify Factors and FactorValues to see what conditions were tested (dose, time, genotype, etc.).
- Measurements and assays
  - Look for Assays: what was measured, using which technology or measurementType, and which Protocol was applied.
  - Check the dataFiles: what data were produced (raw and/or derived), and their formats or identifiers.
- Ontology and terminology
  - Look for OntologyAnnotations and OntologySourceReferences that show standardized terms and where they came from.
- Data and outputs
  - Identify DataFiles and how they relate to the Assays and the Study.
  - See if there are Publications that describe the data or provide access details (repositories, DOIs).
- Provenance and people
  - Note the People/Organizations involved and any Publication links for proper credit and contact points.

Semantic Functions by Object (quick reference)
- Investigation: project-level container with title, description, publications, people, studies, and ontology sources.
- Study: coherent experimental unit with design descriptors, protocols, materials, processes, assays, factors, and any outputs or references.
- Assay: measurement event with type, technology, data files, materials, and its place in a Study’s process sequence.
- Protocol: reusable procedural template with type, description, version, parameters, and components.
- ProtocolParameter: adjustable inputs to a Protocol.
- Process: actual execution of a Protocol on materials; part of a Study’s workflow.
- ProcessParameterValue: concrete values used in a Process step.
- Source: starting biological material with descriptive characteristics.
- Sample: material produced or measured; connected to factors and data.
- Material: inputs/outputs not classified as Source or Sample (e.g., Extract Name); has characteristics.
- MaterialAttribute and MaterialAttributeValue: describe Material properties or conditions.
- Factor and FactorValue: experimental variables and their concrete values.
- Person: individual contributors or contacts.
- Organization: institutions involved (affiliations, facilities).
- Publication: article references tied to the project or studies.
- OntologyAnnotation: controlled-term descriptions used in the metadata.
- OntologySourceReference: source of ontology terms used.
- DataFile: actual data outputs from assays (raw/derived).

Putting it together: how the pieces fit in practice
- The Investigation sets the stage. It lists the Studies and the people/organizations behind the work.
- Each Study describes what was varied, what protocols were used, and what materials were involved. It grounds the experimental design with factors and attributes.
- Each Assay captures a specific measurement, including how it was done and what data were produced. It ties back to the Study and to the samples/materials measured.
- Ontology terms and sources provide consistent vocabulary, while data files and publications connect to the actual results and the broader literature.

If you’re scanning a paper for ISA-style metadata, start with the project description, then trace each Study’s design and protocols, note the materials and samples, map the factors and their values, and finally identify the assays and data outputs. This helps you understand not just what was done, but how to reproduce or reuse the measurements and results.
