What ISA is and why it’s useful
- ISA is a simple, standardized way to describe the metadata of life-science experiments so others can understand, reproduce, and reuse the work.
- It sits on three levels: the big picture (Investigation), the coherent experimental units (Studies), and the actual measurements (Assays).
- By capturing who did what, with what materials, how things were done, and what was measured, ISA helps trace provenance from questions to results.

Key components and how they relate
- Investigation: the project-wide context. It captures the project title, description, people involved, related publications, and a list of Studies. It may also point to vocabulary sources used to describe terms.
- Study: a coherent experimental unit within the project. It has its own title and description, a list of factors (variables like genotype or treatment), protocols used, materials used, and its own set of Assays. It links back to the Investigation.
- Assay: a specific measurement activity within a Study. It records what was measured (measurement type, technology), how it was measured (protocols), what was measured with (materials/samples), and the resulting data files. It links to its Study.

Common objects you’ll see in ISA
- Protocols: a defined, reusable set of steps or procedures that describe how to perform an activity. They provide a standard recipe that can be instantiated in a Process.
- Processes: an actual execution of a Protocol on specific materials under specified conditions. It brings a Protocol to life for particular inputs and outputs.
- Materials (and their subtypes like Source, Sample): physical things used or produced in the study. Materials can have attributes describing properties or provenance.
- Factors (and FactorValues): experimental variables that differ across conditions (e.g., treatment level, genotype). Factor values record the specific condition for a Study or Assay.
- DataFiles: the actual results or datasets produced by an Assay.
- Ontology annotations and ontology source references: controlled vocabulary terms used to describe concepts in a consistent, shareable way.
- People, Organizations, Publications: people involved, the institutions they belong to, and the scholarly publications that describe the work.
- Data provenance and relationships: the schema ties these objects together so you can trace a data file back to a Study, Assay, Protocol, and the conditions under which it was produced.

Semantic Functions by Object
- Investigation: provides the project-level frame—title, description, funding/people, publications, and the set of Studies. It anchors everything else and may reference ontologies.
- Study: defines a coherent experimental unit with its own identity and design. It lists factors, protocols, materials, and the assays that belong to it, and ties back to the Investigation.
- Assay: captures a concrete measurement activity and its outputs. It notes measurement type and technology, links to protocols and materials, and points to data files produced.
- Protocol: a formal, reusable method that can be applied to materials to generate processes. It defines inputs, outputs, and parameters in abstract terms.
- ProtocolParameter: the definitional knobs of a Protocol. It tells you what can be varied in a protocol.
- Process: an instantiated run of a Protocol on particular materials under specific conditions. It has concrete inputs/outputs and parameter values.
- ProcessParameterValue: the actual values used in a Process, giving the exact conditions for that run.
- Source: the origin or supplier of a material; helps trace provenance.
- Sample: a material that is a specific instance used in an Experiment (e.g., a particular tissue sample).
- Material: any physical thing used or produced; may have attributes and links to samples or sources.
- MaterialAttribute / MaterialAttributeValue: describe properties of a Material (e.g., mass, preparation status) and the actual values.
- Factor / FactorValue: the variables that define the experimental design and the specific settings used.
- Person: individual researchers or contributors; connects to organizations and publications.
- Organization: institutions involved in the work.
- Publication: scholarly references describing the work.
- OntologyAnnotation / OntologySourceReference: standardized terms and the sources of those terms that describe concepts in a controlled way.
- DataFile: the concrete data outputs from assays, which can be linked to data repositories or local files.

What to look for in typical life-science articles (a practical checklist)
- Project framing: look for the overall project title and a concise description of what was studied.
- People and affiliations: identify who did the work and which organizations were involved; note corresponding authors if available.
- Studies and design: check how many Studies there are, and what the experimental design looks like (what factors were varied, what conditions were tested).
- Protocols and methods: find descriptions of the procedures used. See if there are named protocols or standard methods that could be reused.
- Materials and samples: identify what materials or samples were used (sources, origins, preparation), and how they were treated.
- Assays and measurements: note what was measured, with what technology or instruments, and over what time points or replicates.
- Data outputs: look for data files or data repositories (where to access results), and any specifications about data formats or metadata.
- Terminology and provenance: check for standardized terms (ontology usage) and references to the sources of those terms.
- Links and provenance: ensure there are connections among Investigation, Studies, Assays, Protocols, Processes, Materials, and DataFiles so you can trace results back to their methods and inputs.

Synthesis with the ISA model (grounded in the schema)
- The ISA schema for Investigation shows it collects core project-level metadata (title, description, publications, people) and a list of Studies, plus optional ontology references. This structure underpins the three-level frame: Investigation → Studies → Assays.
- Each Study can carry its own design details (factors, protocols, materials) and link to one or more Assays, which in turn reference the methods (Protocols) and the concrete materials used.
- Protocols define reusable steps, while Processes instantiate those steps for specific inputs under defined conditions. Factor values and ProcessParameterValues capture the actual experimental settings used in a given run.
- DataFiles capture the results of Assays, and OntologyAnnotations/OntologySourceReferences provide standardized vocabularies to describe concepts consistently.
- In short, ISA is a compact storyboard: who did what, with what materials, how it was done, and what was measured, all tied together so you can reproduce or reuse the data.
