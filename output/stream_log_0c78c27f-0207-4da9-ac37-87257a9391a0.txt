What ISA is and why it’s useful
- ISA stands for Investigation/Study/Assay. It’s a common, machine-friendly way to describe what was done in a life-science project.
- It helps researchers reuse data, compare across studies, and reproduce experiments because metadata are organized in a consistent, explicit way.

Key components and how they relate
- Investigation is the project-level frame: the big title, description, who did it, publications, and the set of Studies it contains.
- Studies are coherent experimental units within the project. Each Study has its own title/description, design notes, and a collection of protocols, materials, processes, and the assays that come from applying them.
- Assays are the concrete measurement activities tied to a Study: what was measured, how, with which materials, and what data files came out.
- The flow often looks like this: Investigation → Studies → Assays. Protocols define steps; Processes instantiate those steps on materials; Factors describe conditions or variables; Data Files store the results.

Common objects you’ll see (what they are and what they carry)
- Protocols: standardized steps or procedures that can be reused across Studies and Assays. They may include parameters and components (parts of the protocol).
- Protocol parameters: the specific settings or choices within a Protocol (the “how” of the steps).
- Processes: actual applications of a Protocol to specific inputs to produce outputs (a Protocol applied to specific samples/materials). Think of a Process as “what happened” in a concrete run.
- Materials: the things used or produced in a Protocol, such as Extracts, samples, or other inputs. They may include details like name and characteristics.
- Source (and Sample): starting biological material (Source) and derived materials or samples. A Sample often represents a material after some processing.
- Data files: the results of an Assay or process, stored in a structured way (e.g., raw data, derived data, images, etc.).
- Factors and Factor values: experimental variables that describe conditions (e.g., genotype, treatment, time). Factor values log the specific settings used.
- Material attributes and Material attribute values: descriptive properties for materials and samples (e.g., organism, tissue type, collection method).
- Ontology annotations and Ontology sources: controlled terms from vocabularies (ontologies) used to name measurement types, units, phenotypes, etc.; OntologySourceReference points to where those terms come from.
- Data about people, organizations, and publications: who did it, where they’re from, and which papers describe the work.
- Publication: the referenced papers or articles; helps link to the scientific record.
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference: how terms are described and where the terms come from.
- Process inputs/outputs: what goes into or comes out of a Process (could be sources, samples, data, materials).

Semantic functions by object
- Investigation
  - Role: the umbrella for the whole project. It records titles, descriptions, people, publications, ontology references, and the set of Studies.
  - Connections: links to Studies, Publications, People, and ontology sources.
  - What it carries: project-level identifiers, overall description, and pointers to the rest of the metadata.
- Study
  - Role: a coherent experimental unit within the Investigation.
  - Connections: contains Protocols, Materials (sources, samples, and other materials), Process sequences, Assays, Factors, and optionally characteristic and unit categories to keep terms consistent.
  - What it carries: study title/description, design descriptors (often as ontology terms), lists of protocols used, materials and outputs, and any study-wide annotations.
- Assay
  - Role: a concrete measurement event tied to a Study.
  - Connections: references a measurement Type (ontology term), a technology type (ontology term), a data file or files, materials (samples or other materials), and a sequence of Processes or Protocols that were applied.
  - What it carries: the specific measurement carried out, how it was done, inputs/outputs (data files, samples, materials), and any assay-level annotations.
- Protocol
  - Role: the recipe or method used in a Study or Assay.
  - Connections: can be reused across Studies/Assays; lists parameters and components.
  - What it carries: name, type (protocol type), description, optional version/URI, and a list of parameter definitions and protocol components.
- Protocol Parameter
  - Role: defined settings within a Protocol (the choices you make when running it).
  - Connections: referenced by a Protocol; described with a parameter name and a value type.
  - What it carries: parameter name (often an ontology term) and its value (when used).
- Process
  - Role: a concrete instance of applying a Protocol to materials to produce outputs.
  - Connections: links to the Protocol it executes; has inputs (sources, samples, data, or other materials) and outputs (new samples, data, or materials).
  - What it carries: date/time, performer, inputs, outputs, and any parameter values used in this run.
- Material, Material Attribute, and Material Attribute Value
  - Material
    - Role: a non-source, non-sample material used or created in a process (e.g., an Extract or a labeled extract).
    - Connections: may have characteristics (attributes); appears in inputs/outputs of Processes.
    - What it carries: name, type (Extract Name, Labeled Extract Name), and a list of characteristics.
  - Material Attribute
    - Role: a category describing a material’s characteristic (e.g., organism, tissue type).
    - Connections: linked to a Material Attribute Value; helps organize attributes consistently.
    - What it carries: a reference to the characteristic type.
  - Material Attribute Value
    - Role: a specific value for a material attribute.
    - Connections: linked to a Material Attribute; may include unit and annotation (ontology term).
    - What it carries: the value, and optionally a unit or an ontology annotation.
- Source and Sample
  - Source
    - Role: starting biological material used in the Study (e.g., tissue, organism).
    - Connections: can have characteristics; may derive downstream samples.
    - What it carries: name and descriptive characteristics.
  - Sample
    - Role: material that results from a Protocol application; used in measurements.
    - Connections: linked to a Study and to Assays that use it; can have factor values and characteristics.
    - What it carries: name, characteristics, factor values, and derived-from relationships.
- Factor and Factor Value
  - Factor
    - Role: an experimental variable (e.g., genotype, treatment) used to describe conditions.
    - Connections: linked to Studies and/or Assays; each factor can have multiple values.
    - What it carries: name and type.
  - Factor Value
    - Role: a specific setting for a Factor in a Study or Assay.
    - Connections: tied to a Factor; may have a value and optional unit.
    - What it carries: the specific value and optional unit.
- Publication, Person, Organization
  - Publication
    - Role: references to papers describing the work, helping trace the source record.
    - Connections: linked from Investigation or Study as relevant.
    - What it carries: bibliographic details (title, authors, identifiers).
  - Person
    - Role: individuals involved; provides contact or author information.
    - Connections: linked to Investigations or Studies.
    - What it carries: name, affiliation, contact details.
  - Organization
    - Role: institutions or groups involved.
    - Connections: linked to Persons and to the Investigation/Study as appropriate.
    - What it carries: organization name and basic details.
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference
  - OntologyAnnotation
    - Role: a term from an ontology used to label a field (like measurement type or unit).
    - Connections: used across many fields to standardize terms.
    - What it carries: the term, optional annotation value or label.
  - OntologySourceReference
    - Role: tells you which ontology source a term comes from.
    - Connections: ties toOntologyAnnotation entries so you can trace the term to its ontology.
    - What it carries: source name, version, and optional file/name.
- DataFile
  - Role: a physical or digital file produced by an Assay or Process.
  - Connections: associated with an Assay; may be linked to a specific data type.
  - What it carries: file name or path, type (Raw/Derived/Data type), and any notes.

What to look for when reading a life-science article (practical checklist)
- Project frame: Is there a clear Investigation title and description? Are the authors and affiliations listed?
- Study-level structure: How many Studies are described? What are their design descriptors (e.g., factors, treatments, time points)?
- Protocols and methods: Are the main protocols described by name and type? Are any protocol parameters defined (with explicit values or ranges)?
- Materials and samples: What sources and samples were used? Are materials described with attributes (e.g., organism, tissue, material type)?
- Experimental variables: What Factors are defined, and what are their values? Are units specified where relevant?
- Data generation: What Assays were performed? What was measured (measurementType) and by what technology (technologyType)?
- Data outputs: What data files are produced? Are their types and names described, with links or references to repositories if possible?
- Ontology terms: Are terms annotated with ontology references (e.g., measurement type, units, sample characteristics) to enable standardization?
- Relationships and flow: Can you trace inputs to processes, then to assays and data files? Are there explicit inputs/outputs for each process?
- Publication and provenance: Are publications listed? Are people and organizations connected to the work clearly described?
- Reuse potential: Are the same Protocols or Materials used in multiple Studies? Are there notes about versioning or annotations that help reuse?

Putting it into practice (quick mental model)
- Think of ISA as a three-layer folder structure: Investigation (the project folder) contains Studies (experiment folders), and each Study contains Assays (individual measurements or experiments). Protocols are the recipes that can be reused, and Processes are concrete executions of those recipes on particular inputs to produce outputs like data files or new samples.
- For each object, ask: What is its role? What does it connect to? What information does it carry (inputs, outputs, values, terms, identifiers)?
- Use ontology terms to name things consistently, and note where terms come from (ontology sources) to keep terms interoperable with other work.

Semantic Functions by Object (one- to two-sentence summaries)
- Investigation: The project-wide container that holds the overall description, people, publications, ontology sources, and the set of Studies.
- Study: A coherent experimental unit within the project, documenting design, protocols, materials, processes, assays, factors, and derived outputs.
- Assay: A specific measurement event within a Study, detailing what was measured, how, and with which data outputs.
- Protocol: The reusable method or procedure used in Studies/Assays; includes parameters and components.
- ProtocolParameter: A defined setting within a Protocol; specifies how the Protocol can be customized.
- Process: A concrete execution of a Protocol on particular inputs to produce outputs; carries input/output relationships and timing/performer context.
- ProcessParameterValue: The actual values used in a Process for its parameters.
- Source: The starting biological material used in the Study.
- Sample: A material produced or used in a Protocol, used in measurements.
- Material: Non-source, non-sample material (like an extract) used or produced in a Process; carries its own characteristics.
- MaterialAttribute and MaterialAttributeValue: Categories and specific values describing material characteristics; help keep descriptions consistent.
- Factor and FactorValue: Experimental variables and their specific values used in the Study or Assay.
- Person and Organization: People involved and their institutions; provide provenance and contact references.
- Publication: The paper or reference describing the work; helps connect metadata to the literature.
- OntologyAnnotation and OntologySourceReference: Controlled terms and their sources used to label fields in the ISA model.
- DataFile: The actual data artifacts produced by Assays or Processes; represent the outputs of the work.
