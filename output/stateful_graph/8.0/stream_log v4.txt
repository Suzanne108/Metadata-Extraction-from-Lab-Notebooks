   Content: ISA is a practical way to describe life-science experiments so others can understand, reuse, and combine the data. It organizes metadata in a consistent, machine-readable way without requiring the reader to guess what was done.

What ISA captures at a high level
- It splits project information into three linked layers: Investigation, Studies, and Assays.
- Investigation covers the project-wide context: title, description, people, publications, and the ontology terms used to describe things.
- Studies describe coherent units of work within the project: what was done, with which materials, and under which design.
- Assays record the actual measurements or observations made in a study: what was measured, with what technique, and what data were produced.

Key components and how they relate
- Investigation
  - The top-level container for a project.
  - Holds overall description, responsible people, publications, and references to controlled terms (ontology sources).
  - Contains one or more Studies.
- Studies
  - The individual experimental units within the project.
  - Include a title and description, design descriptors, protocols used, materials (samples and other materials), processes, and any assays tied to the study.
  - List factors (experimental variables) and their values, which helps explain results.
  - Point to associated data (via data files) produced by the assays.
- Assays
  - The concrete measurement activities within a study.
  - Include the type of measurement (measurement type), the technology used (technology type), data files produced, and any protocols or materials involved.
  - May reference the sequence of processing steps (processes) and the materials used (samples, extracts, or other materials).

Common objects you’ll see in ISA
- Protocols
  - Reusable, defined procedures or sets of steps used in experiments.
  - May include parameters; serve as the blueprint for how to perform parts of a study.
- Processes
  - Actual execution steps drawn from protocols, arranged in a sequence to show the workflow.
- Materials
  - The physical things used or produced: samples, extracts, and other materials.
  - In ISA, materials are described in a way that supports linking to the right samples or extracts.
- Factors
  - Experimental variables (e.g., genotype, treatment, time point).
  - Help explain differences in results across samples or conditions.
- Data files
  - The results from assays; where the measurements are stored or linked.
- Ontology annotations
  - Use controlled terms from standard vocabularies to describe concepts (e.g., sample type, treatment).
  - Include term sources and accession references to keep descriptions consistent and searchable.

A simple mental model
- Think of ISA as a three-layer folder: Investigation is the project folder, Studies are the experiment folders inside it, and Assays are the measurement files inside each study.
- Protocols are the recipe books you reuse across studies.
- Materials are the physical items you work with (samples, extracts, etc.).
- Factors are the knobs you turn (and record) to show what was varied.
- Data files are the results you collect, tied to the corresponding assay.
- Ontology annotations are the shared vocabulary that keeps terms consistent across papers.

What to look for in typical life-science articles (a practical checklist)
- Project scope and context
  - Clear project title and a concise description.
  - Author list with affiliations and, if available, contact details.
  - References to publications or data sources that underpin the work.
- Study-level details
  - Each study has a distinct title/description.
  - A description of the experimental design (what variables were tested).
  - A list of factors and the values used (e.g., genotype, treatment, time point).
  - Protocols used in the study (named protocols or described steps).
  - Materials used (samples, extracts, or other materials) and how they were prepared.
  - Any sequence of steps (processes) that document the workflow.
- Assay and data
  - The type of measurement or observation (data types like sequencing, imaging, etc.).
  - The technology or method used to collect data (instrument or assay type).
  - Data files produced or linked (where to access the raw or processed results).
  - How the data relate to the study (which assay produced which results).
- terminology and standardization
  - Look for ontology terms or controlled vocabulary used to describe factors, materials, or measurements.
  - Notes about term sources or accession numbers that identify the vocabulary used.
- Reproducibility and access
  - Any links to protocols, raw data, or data repositories.
  - A description of how to reproduce the work or how to access the data for reanalysis.

Short, practical takeaway
- ISA provides a clear, repeatable structure to describe experiments: project context (Investigation), experimental units (Studies), and measurements (Assays).
- Expect to see linked protocols, described materials and factors, and data files that result from specific assays.
- Use ontology terms to keep descriptions consistent across papers and datasets.
